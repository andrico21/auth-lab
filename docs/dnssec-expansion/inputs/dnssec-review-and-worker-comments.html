2. Required corrections

DNS-R01 — Validating-stub proof is silently promoted into resolver trust

Reproduce: lab-dnssec05, validating-stub; advance through stub-owns-independent-anchor-and-validation, record-selected-evidence and accepted-use-and-cache-boundary. These are source indexes 81, 87 and 90 in the reviewed bytes. Inspect actor, exact holdings and the selected cache.

The stub owns stub-public-v1 and checks with its configured anchor. The resolver’s resolver-fetch-only-v1 contains no anchors. Nevertheless, the shared completion path creates a stub-authored witness at dnsResolver, then stores a secure cache there with checkingOwner: dnsStub and trustStore: stub-public-v1. No message transfers the stub decision back: there are zero messages after its check. The delivered resolver response correctly had AD=0.

Code: src/dnssec-network-cases.js, context selection at 74–76 and 103–104, shared completion at 433–458. The witness declares creator: dnsStub, while its acquisition is a resolver-local create operation. This is an actual state/holding error, not merely a caption.

Basis: the independent validating stub has its own trust relationship and performs its own checks; its upstream fetch service does not automatically inherit the result. See RFC 4033 §7, RFC 4035 §§4.9–5, and the plan’s DNS05/custody requirements. The RFC does not prescribe this application’s cache API; the violation is the model’s invented transfer of trust across its declared actors.

Change: make result/evidence/cache ownership explicit in the completion path. Keep accepted evidence and any authenticated reusable cache at dnsStub for this case. A separately modeled raw resolver cache can remain unvalidated in the resolver’s own namespace. Correct event actors, creator/acquire operations, derivation references, bindings, protocol state and exact histories together. Do not invent a reverse verdict packet or give the resolver an anchor to make the old trace pass: that changes the lesson.

Close with: before the stub check, no stub acceptance or authenticated cache; after it, stub-owned proof/cache in stub-public-v1; no resolver acquisition of unsent local evidence. Rewind removes the corresponding holdings, and full/field/exact-object views agree at equal prefixes. Preserve the distinct owners in trusted-resolver, local-validator, upstream-validator and both-validator cases. Add a cross-actor create/derivation assertion that would have caught this defect.

Expected-authority repair is required here: the effective worksheet’s validating-stub checkpoint accepted-use-and-cache-boundary explicitly names dnsResolver as its actor while binding the cache to the stub’s checking context. Correct that actor and audit the case’s inherited ownership/cache declarations through an independently justified additive erratum. The existing expectation helps explain why the current contract gate accepts the wrong location; do not preserve it merely to keep the old gate green. Preserve unchanged public numerical tuples.

DNS-R02 — Warm response has two contradictory TTLs

Reproduce: lab-dnssec05, response-flags-and-cached-ad; inspect exact lab-dnssec05:stub:r-message and its returned A record.

Field in the same outgoing occurrence Actual
A RRset TTL 240 seconds
Remaining cache lifetime 240 seconds
Message receivedRrsetTtl 300 seconds
Message receivedRrsigTtl 300 seconds

Code: src/dnssec-network-cases.js:167 gives every nonempty response fixed 300-second metadata; 447–448 calculate the remaining lifetime and 463–467 return the aged answer. The signed Original TTL is correctly separate.

Basis: cache aging and signature validity are separate, and authenticated RRset/RRSIG TTLs have bounded lifetimes (RFC 4033 §8.1, RFC 4035 §5.3.3). Irrespective of downstream behavior, exact packet inspection must not give two different received TTLs for one occurrence.

Change: derive the outgoing occurrence’s TTL metadata from the actual records and deadlines. Model the accompanying RRSIG’s ordinary TTL independently from signed RDATA; preserve Original TTL and signature bytes. Do not claim a received signature TTL when no signature is carried. Review the NTA response’s 299-versus-300 metadata through the same helper, keeping its unverified-data semantics explicit.

Close with: the warm occurrence consistently reports 240; signed Original TTL remains 3600; the earlier receipt/deadline stays unchanged. Add DO=0/no-RRSIG and diagnostic/NTA controls. Test the exact emitted packet and held representation, not just the high-level cache window.

DNS-R03 — Fresh NXDOMAIN cache entries cannot be reused under the correct key

Affected: DNS07/nxdomain, DNS07/wildcard-excluded, DNS08/nsec3-nxdomain.

Code: src/dnssec-denial-cases.js:308 forces negativeKind: null; src/dnssec-denial.js:81 therefore includes QTYPE. The independently seeded DNS10/negative-cache-hit at denial-cases 132–136 uses the correct key, masking the insertion/reuse mismatch.

For DNS07/nxdomain the actual entry key is ["public-v1","resolver-public-v1","no.lab.example.","A","IN"]. The appropriate NXDOMAIN key omits "A". Calling the application’s own dnssecDenialCacheEligible with the freshly created entry and an NXDOMAIN context returns false immediately, for both A and AAAA. Time is still valid: receipt/current clock 1792713600, expiry 1792713900. The separate DNS10 entry passes both queries.

Basis: RFC 2308 §5 distinguishes QNAME/QCLASS name-error reuse from QNAME/QTYPE/QCLASS type-absence reuse. This is a named-case state inconsistency; selecting one lesson then another does not transfer a live cache, and the review does not suggest otherwise.

Change: derive key shape from the validated negative result. Preserve the view/trust namespace, type-specific NODATA, failure checking context and compact-denial/CO distinctions. Do not normalize all negative-like outcomes to one key.

Close with: feed each actual newly inserted entry back into eligibility at the insertion clock and a later valid clock. It must answer the same name/class for another type without restarting TTL. NODATA must remain type-specific; expiry and incompatible view/trust-store queries must fail. Keep all 159 named cases; these can be bounded additional unit fixtures.

DNS-R04 — Denial evaluator lacks the ancestor-delegation restriction

Scope: exported helper; no existing selectable UI bypass was found. DNS07/proof-from-wrong-zone remains correctly rejected by its caller-supplied requiredZone.

Reproduce: give dnssecCheckDenial the existing signed denial-parent-unsigned-lab NSEC and its signature, qname lab.example., zone example., key example-zsk-g1, fixture clock, and QTYPE A, AAAA or CNAME. The authenticated bitmap is NS RRSIG NSEC, with ancestor signer example.. All three return valid: true, kind: nodata, reason: authenticated-type-absence. The DS-absence control succeeds as expected.

Code: exact-owner branch src/dnssec-denial.js:49; audit the analogous matching-NSEC3 branch at 69 too. Only the NSEC counterexample was executed in this review.

Basis: RFC 6840 §4.1 forbids ancestor-delegation NSEC/NSEC3 from denying non-DS records at the cut, or records below it. NS/SOA bits and signer authority are substantive checks; a fixture name is not a substitute.

Change: enforce this authority restriction inside denial evaluation before exact-owner/hash absence succeeds. Keep requiredZone as an additional caller-context guard. Audit the related DNAME and NSEC3 closest-encloser restrictions against the primary rules; do not claim an unexecuted hashed counterexample has already been reproduced.

Close with: unchanged parent proof must allow DS absence but reject A/AAAA/CNAME absence; valid child-side NODATA and existing wrong-zone tests remain correct. Use independently sourced signed fixtures if adding the analogous NSEC3 tests.

DNS-R05 — Algorithm retirement starts its cache drain from the wrong publication

Affected lesson: DNS14/dual-algorithm-rollover. The existing selected dual-capable validator succeeds; the defect is the advertised safe retirement timeline.

Code: src/dnssec-signing-cases.js:334–341. The parent publishes the overlap DS set for algorithms 13 and 15 at T+4500. The lesson declares old DS dependencies ended at T+91500, but the old algorithm’s DS remains published until T+95100. At that same later time it removes the old DS, ordinary signatures and DNSKEY.

Executed counterexample: an additional reviewer profile supports algorithm 13 but not 15. It receives the still-authoritative overlap DS at T+95099; its 86400-second TTL reaches T+181499. At T+95101, the candidate’s algorithm-15-only child yields bogus with that cached supported DS, while a fresh algorithm-15-only DS yields insecure for this profile. The existing signature tuples and actual evaluator reproduce both results. This is an added capability/cache cohort, not an existing named failure.

Basis: RFC 6781 §4.1.4 describes old-DS withdrawal, cache draining and later child-key/signature withdrawal. It is operational guidance, not a new claim that a validator must validate every advertised algorithm. The plan requires cache-aware algorithm rollover; waiting since overlap addition cannot expire DS data that is still being published.

Change: separate old-DS removal, parent convergence, last possible old-DS cache expiry and child-key/signature retirement. Calculate bounds from the removal/convergence event and the relevant TTLs, then respect any subsequent child DNSKEY/data dependencies. Alternatively expose the current simultaneous removal as an explicitly failing profile, while retaining a correct successful rollover.

Close with: test a resolver that receives the old-containing DS just before withdrawal/convergence, a fresh resolver and a dual-capable resolver. Show each exact cache receipt/deadline and supported path. Neither a single selected cache captured early nor the all-algorithms-capable profile closes this correction. Revise independent expected timelines additively if needed.

DNS-R06 — Operator policy is mislabeled as implementation capability

Reproduce: DNS14/sha1-signing-disabled. State reports implementationValidationSupportRequired: true, implementationCapable: [13,15], operatorTreatAs: unsupported, but unsupportedReason: implementation-capability. modern-and-obsolete-paths uses the same misleading reason. The ultimate insecure/secure classifications in these cases are not themselves the reported defect.

Code: src/dnssec-publication.js:95–100 derives implementationCapable from policy.validationAlgorithms, which is the operator acceptance list; the shared reason consequently collapses distinct controls. The lesson already has the registry columns and operator exclusion list to teach the distinction correctly.

Basis: RFC 9905 §2 distinguishes continued implementation validation support for algorithms 5/7 from the operator requirement to treat them as unsupported. The original plan explicitly calls out this distinction. A registry implementation requirement is also not proof of a particular installed engine’s capabilities.

Change: represent registry recommendations/requirements, declared implementation capability, publisher policy and operator acceptance independently. Choose the rejection reason from the actual cause. If the fixed mathematical catalog only contains 13/15, label that as fixture coverage, not as the DNSSEC implementation policy. Do not enable SHA-1 signing or operator acceptance to repair a label.

Close with: SHA-1 operator exclusion reports policy as the cause; the deliberately unsupported algorithm case reports actual capability limits; modern usable paths remain usable. Keep DS-digest policy and NSEC3 hashing separate.

DNS-R09 — Bring the CSYNC distinction into DNS16

The DNS16 specification requires a separate explanation that CSYNC requests NS/glue synchronization and does not itself request DS replacement. The generic CSYNC field metadata at src/dnssec-fields.js:2832 says this correctly, but no DNS16 case steps, introduction or chapter content references it. All ten cases were inspected. An unused field description is not a contextual lesson explanation.

Change: add a short, directly reachable explanation/comparison within DNS16. Reuse the existing field definition and source. Explain the different record purpose and parent decision without inventing a CSYNC authorization to change DS. A small lesson panel or explanatory step is sufficient; a 23rd lesson or an extra named-case family is not requested. RFC 7477 supplies the CSYNC semantics.

Close with: a learner entering ordinary DNS16 can reach the distinction without searching the entire field catalog; source and emitted content checks verify it. If an actual transfer/processing step is added, give its records and decisions real ownership and state instead of a decorative packet.

3. Nonblocking clarity improvements

C01 — Readable verdicts. The main panel prints identifiers such as dnsValidator and complete-anchor-ds-dnskey-answer-path. Use actor display names and concise explanations; retain identifiers in expandable details. DNS03 proved-no-ds should explain that authenticated unsigned-delegation evidence produces an insecure result and this selected secure-only application refuses it. This is different from saying the DNS answer was forged.

C02 — Scope the receipt label. DNS05’s warm-cache case ends with “Data received No” and “Application received answer.” The first means no fresh resolver authority receipt. Qualify it and display cache reuse/stub delivery separately. Do not invent a fresh fetch or fresh check to make the words agree.

C03 — Declare DNS12’s revalidation policy. old-key-removed-too-early caches an already-secure answer, then revalidates it against a fresh keyset and returns SERVFAIL while 3400 seconds remain. Revalidation is not categorically forbidden by the cited rollover guidance; this is not counted as an RFC violation. Explain why this fixture rechecks already accepted cached data, or use stale, unvalidated upstream data to illustrate the missing-old-key problem. Contrast ordinary reuse of an unexpired accepted cache so learners do not infer that withdrawing a published key instantly revokes every cached answer.

The planned hidden-tag/ranked-search work is already the right place to address noisy broad searches. It is not a new DNSSEC defect or a reason to duplicate that assignment here.
